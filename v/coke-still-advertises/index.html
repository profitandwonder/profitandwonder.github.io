Why does Coca-Cola still advertise if everyone knows it?

Everyone knows Coca-Cola. Yet it spent $5.4 billion on ads in 2025. Why?

Because buyers forget. Other brands push it out of your head. Ads keep it the first drink you think of. That's mental availability.

Brands that stop advertising pay for it. In one study of 57 cases of drink brands that went dark, sales fell about 16 percent after one year, and 25 percent after two, on average. Big brands fell less than small ones. A 2023 US replication found the same direction.

The other half is physical availability: being easy to buy. Coke's aim, attributed to its 1923 boss Robert Woodruff, was to be "within arm's reach of desire". Today it sells in more than 200 countries.

Brands grow two ways: easy to think of, and easy to buy.

Receipts

Where the facts in this video come from.

  1. $5.4 billion on ads in 2025 The Coca-Cola Company, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025 (filed 2026): "Advertising expenses for 2025 and 2024 were $5.4 billion and $5.1 billion, respectively." Source: sec.gov
  2. Mental and physical availability Byron Sharp, How Brands Grow (2010), Ehrenberg-Bass Institute. Brands grow by being easy to think of in buying situations and easy to buy.
  3. −16% after 1 year, −25% after 2 years Hartnett, Gelzinis, Beal, Kennedy & Sharp, "When Brands Go Dark", Journal of Advertising Research 61(3), 2021. 57 cases of brands in one Australian consumer goods category (beer, cider, spirits) that stopped all mass-media advertising for a year or more, over 20+ years of data. Ehrenberg-Bass summary: "on average, sales fell 16% after one year, and 25% after two years". Source: marketingscience.info
  4. Big brands fell less than small ones in that study. A 2023 replication (365 US brands, 22 categories, JAR) found market share fell ~10% after one year and ~20% after two, so the direction holds.
  5. Sold in more than 200 countries today The Coca-Cola Company, Form 10-K for 2025, Item 1: Coke products "are now sold in more than 200 countries and territories". Source: sec.gov
  6. "Within arm's reach of desire" attributed to Robert W. Woodruff, Coca-Cola president from 1923 (Advertising Hall of Fame). Source: advertisinghalloffame.org
