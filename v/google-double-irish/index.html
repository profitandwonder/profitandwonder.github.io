What was Google's Double Irish with a Dutch Sandwich?

When a business in Europe bought Google ads, the money was booked in Google Ireland. Google Ireland then paid a big royalty, a fee to use Google's search technology, to Google Netherlands Holdings. That Dutch company had no employees.

The Dutch company passed almost all of the money on to Google Ireland Holdings. It was Irish on paper, but tax resident in Bermuda, where companies pay no corporate income tax. Two Irish slices with a Dutch filling: the "sandwich". In 2018, €21.8 billion went this way. In 2019 the Bermuda company made about $13 billion in profit.

Every step was legal. Ireland closed the route to new companies from 2015, but let existing users carry on until the end of 2020. Google stopped in December 2019. Google Ireland paid €574.5 million of Irish tax on its 2024 accounts.

How it worked (2010): ad sales in Europe, Middle East and Africa are booked in Google Ireland, where little profit is shown; money goes on to Google Netherlands Holdings, "just a shell (it has no employees)", which passes on about 99.8% to Bermuda; Bermuda has no corporate income tax 2010-10-22 Source: [Bloomberg Businessweek via NBC News]
  2. Why the Dutch step: Irish law "makes it difficult for Google to send the money directly to Bermuda without incurring a large tax hit"; going via the Netherlands avoids "triggering US income taxes or European withholding taxes" 2010-10-22; 2016-02-19 Sources: nbcnews.com, [Irish Times]
  3. The three companies: Google Ireland Limited (sells the ads), Google Netherlands Holdings BV (the middle), Google Ireland Holdings Unlimited Company (Irish-registered, tax resident in Bermuda, holds the right to license Google's IP outside the US) 2018-01-02 Source: [Irish Times]

  5. 2017: €19.9bn moved through the Dutch company to Bermuda; 2018: €21.8bn (Dutch Chamber of Commerce filings seen by Reuters) 2020-01-02 Source: dutchnews.nl
