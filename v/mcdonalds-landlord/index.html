Does McDonald's make more money from rent than from burgers?

Yes, if you compare rent with the food its own restaurants sell. In 2025, the people who run McDonald's restaurants paid McDonald's $10.4 billion in rent. The restaurants McDonald's runs itself sold $9.7 billion of food.

The idea came from Harry Sonneborn, the company's money man, in 1956. Under its first deal, McDonald's kept 1.4 cents of each dollar a restaurant sold. So Sonneborn had McDonald's take the land, and rent it to each owner for more.

A banker is said to have refused him a loan, because nobody lends to a burger business. Sonneborn's reported answer: we are in the real estate business.

Today the owner pays for the fryers and the sign. McDonald's keeps the land and the building, at about half of its restaurants. The rent is a share of each restaurant's sales.

The fine print: "landlord" is our word. Rent is money in, not profit.

Receipts

Where the facts in this video come from.

  1. Franchisees paid McDonald's $10.4 billion in rent in 2025 FY2025 10-K, note "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53
  2. The restaurants McDonald's runs itself sold $9.7 billion of food, less than the rent in F1 FY2025 10-K, Statement of Income, p. 39
  3. In a standard franchise McDonald's owns or leases the land and building; the franchisee pays for the kitchen, signs, seats and decor, then pays rent and a royalty as a percent of sales, with a minimum rent; the deal runs about 20 years FY2025 10-K, Item 1 and p. 53
  4. McDonald's owns about 56% of the land and 80% of the buildings (consolidated markets); it rents the rest and rents it on 2025-12-31 10-K, MD&A p. 19-20
  5. Only "conventional" franchises pay rent: 22,570 restaurants, about half 2025-12-31 10-K, p. 43
  6. Ray Kroc's first contract gave him a $950 fee per store and 1.9% of its sales; 0.5% went to the McDonald brothers, so he kept 1.4% 1955 Love (1986); Kroc (1977) confirms the split and says expenses ate the fees
  7. Harry Sonneborn's idea: a new company, Franchise Realty Corporation, leases the site for 20 years and sublets the finished store to the franchisee at a markup 1956 Love, ch. "Making Money", pp. 153-155
  8. The markup was 20%, then 40%: a site that cost McDonald's $600 a month cost the franchisee at least $840 late 1950s Love, pp. 154-155
  9. Sonneborn to a New York banker who refused to lend to a food business: "We are not basically in the food business. We are in the real estate business. The only reason we sell fifteen-cent hamburgers is because they are the greatest producer of revenue from which our tenants can pay us our rent." said about 1961; printed 1986 Love, ch. "The Buyout", about p. 200
  10. F1 Rent franchisees paid McDonald's $10,442 million in rent in fiscal 2025. McDonald's Form 10-K FY2025 (SEC, filed 2026-02-24), note "Franchise Arrangements", p. 53.
  11. F3 Burgers the restaurants McDonald's runs itself sold $9,690 million in fiscal 2025 (same 10-K, Statement of Income). "From selling burgers" means these sales; the description says so. Rent is money in, not profit.
  12. F5 The deal McDonald's owns or leases the land and building; the franchisee pays for equipment, signs, seating and decor; rent is a percent of sales with a minimum (same 10-K, Item 1). About half of all McDonald's pay rent this way (F7, in the description).
  13. F11 1.4 cents Kroc's 1955 contract: 1.9% of a store's sales, 0.5% of it to the McDonald brothers. John F. Love, "McDonald's: Behind the Arches" (1986); Ray Kroc, "Grinding It Out" (1977), who says expenses ate the fees ("could barely pay its bills" is our wording of that).
  14. F12, F13 The land Franchise Realty Corporation, 1956: lease the site, sublet it to the franchisee at a markup of 20%, then 40% (Love, pp. 153-155). "Took the land" = took the lease on it; today it owns about 56% of the land (F6).
  15. F14 The banker and the line Love (1986), ch. "The Buyout", about p. 200, a retelling written 25 years later. The line is spoken as a paraphrase, not a quote; the book's words are "We are not basically in the food business. We are in the real estate business. The only reason we sell fifteen-cent hamburgers is because they are the greatest producer of revenue from which our tenants can pay us our rent." We do not say the meeting won the loan, so the loan stays open on purpose.
