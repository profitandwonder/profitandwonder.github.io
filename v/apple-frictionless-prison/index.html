Why is it so hard to leave Apple?

Apple's trick is two doors. On the way in, it takes away every bit of friction. The 1998 iMac had a handle, an invitation to touch it. The white earbuds turned every user into a walking ad. Even the box was designed to feel like a gift.

On the way out, the friction stays. To set up an Apple Watch, you hold it next to an unlocked iPhone. Texts from Android phones show up green, and iMessage still isn't on Android.

Apple's own people saw it. In 2016, an email to Phil Schiller, Apple's marketing boss, called iMessage "serious lock-in". He replied that putting it on Android "will hurt us more than help us."

Of iPhone buyers in the year to June 2025, 89% already had an iPhone. Most chose to stay. But next time something feels effortless, ask how hard it would be to leave.
