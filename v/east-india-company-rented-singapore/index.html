How did modern Singapore start?

Modern Singapore started as a business deal. In 1819 the East India Company, a British company owned by shareholders, with its own armies, wanted a base nearby. The Dutch taxed British ships in their ports.

The Sultan of Johor was backed by the Dutch and would not deal. So the company's man, Stamford Raffles, recognised the Sultan's older brother as Sultan instead. The company paid two local rulers 8,000 Spanish dollars a year to keep a trading post.

In 1824 a new treaty gave the company the whole island, for 60,000 Spanish dollars and monthly payments for life.

After a revolt in India in 1857, the British Crown took the company's power. Singapore became a Crown colony in 1867.

Singapore became an independent republic on 9 August 1965. That year it made about $517 per person, about $5,300 in today's money (our sum). In 2025 it made about $98,900.
