Who really gets paid at a Crumbl cookie shop?

Crumbl sells giant, sugary cookies in a pink box, with a new menu every week. Fans film themselves tasting each new flavour, so it grew less like a bakery and more like a weekly show.

Crumbl does not run most of its shops. Local owners pay to use the name and the recipes (a franchise). Crumbl then takes about 10 cents of every dollar they sell: an 8% royalty plus a 2% marketing fee. That cut comes off sales, before rent, staff and flour.

A show needs a new episode every week, and there are only so many flavours. People tried it, shrugged, and stopped coming back: visits fell 32% in a year. Crumbl's cut still came first. In September 2026 the owner in the middle kept $1,832 for the month.

One married couple in Kentucky closed their shop and filed for Chapter 7 bankruptcy in October 2026, with more than $352,000 of debt and about $10,000 of assets.

Our view: nobody here is a villain. The hype paid for the first visit; only the cookie could pay for the second. Crumbl was paid on the first visits. The owners needed the second ones.
