Does Microsoft owe you money when Outlook goes down?

Business email from Microsoft comes with a written promise (an SLA): it works 99.9% of the time. That allows about 43 minutes down in a month.

On 12 November 2026 an upgrade went wrong, and Outlook business email was down for about a day, in more than 70 countries. So the promise pays out.

The pay-back is small: up to half of the email part of one month's bill. Office Watch's example: a two-person business paying $28 a month gets at most $14.

It is not cash. It is money off a future bill, and only if the customer sends a claim with proof by 31 December 2026. The contract calls that credit the "sole and exclusive remedy": lost sales and staff time are not covered. Home plans are not covered at all.

How to claim (Office Watch): there is no public form. An admin opens a support request in the Microsoft 365 admin center (Help & support, then Contact Support) and quotes incident EX1550543. A firm that buys through a reseller asks the reseller.

Our view: the hassle is the point. Few small firms will do the paperwork for $14, and Microsoft has more than 450 million paying work accounts. Every claim nobody sends is money it keeps.
