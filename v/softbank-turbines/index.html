Why did SoftBank lose $3.7 billion on gas turbines it never got?

AI data centres can use as much power as a city. Connecting one to the power grid can take years, so many data centres bring their own gas turbines (giant engines that burn gas to make electricity).

Turbines are now sold out for years. The biggest seller, GE Vernova, takes money up front for a place in its queue. SoftBank feared being stuck at the back, so it booked more places than its giant Ohio campus needed.

Then it decided it needed fewer and gave the spare places back. In November 2026 it booked a $3.7 billion loss: about $2.9 billion of advance payments it cannot get back, and about $0.9 billion for contract changes.

Elon Musk posted that SoftBank was "defaulting". SoftBank's CFO said: "There is no such default."

The fine print: reports say most of the places were with GE Vernova, not all.
