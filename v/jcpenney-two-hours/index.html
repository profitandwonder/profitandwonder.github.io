Why did Google punish J.C. Penney?

For months in 2011, a Google search for Samsonite carry-on luggage put J.C. Penney first, ahead of Samsonite itself.

A New York Times reporter looked into it. He found 2,015 pages linking to Penney's website, many on sites about something else entirely.

He took it to Matt Cutts, head of Google's web spam team. Cutts said: "I can confirm that this violates our guidelines." Penney said it never authorized those links.

Google acted anyway. At 7 p.m., Penney was number one for that luggage search. Two hours later, it was number seventy-one. Nobody shops that far down.

Google still bans links like these. Its spam policies list links made mainly to manipulate search rankings.
