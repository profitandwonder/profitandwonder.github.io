Why does Caffè Nero show a loss when it sells so much coffee?

In 2007 Caffè Nero was taken private with borrowed money. The debt did not stay with the buyer. It sat on the coffee company itself.

Interest is a cost, and tax is charged on profit. In the year to May 2025 the group made £32.3m of operating profit. After the interest, it showed a £41.3m loss before tax. These group figures cover all its countries, not only the UK. Nero says it has been loss making for UK corporation tax purposes.

A 2018 report also found a £98.4m loan from Nero's own parent company, at 25% interest. The interest was added to the loan, which reached £235m by 2017. Nero says it never took UK tax deductions for shareholder loans, and that its losses come from interest owed to banks.

It is all legal. Since 2017 the UK caps interest deductions at 30% of a group's taxable earnings. In the year to May 2026 Nero had record group sales of £670m across 1,191 stores.
