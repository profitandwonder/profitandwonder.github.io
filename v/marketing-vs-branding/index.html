What is the difference between marketing and branding?

Marketing is everything that gets you to buy: the product, the price, where it's sold, and the ads.

Branding is what you remember: the name, the colour, the feeling. It's why you pick the same one again next time.

So branding is one part of marketing. And one ad can do both jobs. But it mostly works one way.

System1 tests TV ads by measuring how viewers respond. Across its database of over 20,000 UK TV ads, 98% of the ads rated highest for brand building also scored above average for quick sales. Going the other way, three in five ads that scored well for quick sales scored low for brand.

These are test scores, not measured sales. Still, the pattern is clear: strong brand ads tend to sell too, and most quick-sale ads don't build the brand.

Marketing gets you the sale. Branding gets you the next one.

Receipts

Where the facts in this video come from.

  1. Marketing (definition) American Marketing Association: "Marketing is the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large." "Product, price, where it's sold, and the ads" is a plain-English version of creating, exchanging, delivering and communicating. Source: ama.org
  2. Brand (definition) AMA Dictionary: "A name, term, design, symbol, or any other feature that identifies one seller's good or service as distinct from those of other sellers." "What you remember" follows Keller's customer-based brand equity: the brand is what is stored in the buyer's memory (Keller, Journal of Marketing 57(1), 1993; still the standard textbook view in Keller & Swaminathan, Strategic Brand Management, 5th ed., 2019). Source: marketing-dictionary.org
  3. "Branding is one part of marketing" the common textbook view (the AMA's marketing definition includes "communicating" and creating offerings; the brand is one feature of the offering). Some people use "brand" for the whole company identity, which is the grey area.
  4. Proof (System1, 15 June 2023) "97.9% of ads that land in the 5-Star range … also achieve above average Spike." "Three in five ads (60.3%) with above average Spike only score 1- or 2-Stars." Star = predicted long-term brand building; Spike = predicted short-term sales effect over the 8–10 weeks after airing. Database: over 20,000 UK TV ads. Also reported by Mark Ritson, Marketing Week, 1 Feb 2023. Source: system1group.com
