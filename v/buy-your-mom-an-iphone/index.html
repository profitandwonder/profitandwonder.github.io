Why do green text bubbles keep people on iPhone?

On an iPhone, a text from an Android phone shows up green. In 2022, The Wall Street Journal reported on teenagers who dreaded that green bubble.

That same year, at a tech conference, a man told Tim Cook his mom couldn't see his videos, because she had an Android. Cook's answer: "Buy your mom an iPhone." Customer support, in five words.

Two years later, in iOS 18, Apple added RCS, a newer texting standard. Texts with Android got full-size photos, read receipts and typing dots. The bubbles stayed green.

By spring 2025, 88% of US teens surveyed owned an iPhone. The survey doesn't ask why, so here is our reading: Apple doesn't need a lock on the door, because your friends hold the key.
