What is a bond, and why do bond prices fall when rates rise?

A bond is an IOU. You lend a government (or a company) money. It pays you a fixed amount every year, and it gives the full amount back at the end.

You don't have to wait for the end. You can sell your bond to someone else on the bond market. But its price moves. If new bonds pay more than yours, nobody pays full price for your old one. If new bonds pay less, yours is worth more. It's a seesaw: rates up, old bond prices down.

So you only lose if you sell while rates are up. Hold it to the end and you still get the full amount back.

Governments ride the same seesaw. In the 2026 financial year, the US paid more in net interest ($1,051.8B) than on national defense ($965.2B).
