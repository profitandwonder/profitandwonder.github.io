How did Starbucks UK pay so little tax?

Back in 2012, Starbucks' British shops paid a fee of 6% of sales to a sister company in Amsterdam, for the right to use the Starbucks name. Starbucks owned both sides: left pocket paying right pocket.

A fee counts as a cost, so the profit in Britain shrank on paper, and so did the tax. For every £100 spent, Starbucks paid under 30p of corporation tax. KFC paid over £3. From 1998 to 2012, Starbucks paid £8.6 million of tax on more than £3 billion of UK sales. It was legal.

After the outcry, Starbucks paid £20 million of tax it did not legally owe. In 2014 it moved the brand company to London. The fee is still paid today, £43.2 million in the year to September 2025, but it now lands in a London company that pays UK tax.
