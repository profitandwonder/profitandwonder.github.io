Why did NBC cut off the end of the 1968 Jets v Raiders game?

In 1968 one company often paid for a whole TV show and ran only its own ads in it. Timex, the watch company, bought the two-hour slot for the film Heidi on NBC. Its contract said Heidi starts at 7 pm Eastern, and no delay "for any reason".

That Sunday the Jets v Raiders game ran long, with lots of penalties and injuries. The NBC bosses, watching at home, changed their minds: keep the game on. But the phone lines were jammed by fans calling to ask if the game was still on. Supervisor Dick Cline heard nothing, so he cut to Heidi with 65 seconds left and the Jets ahead 32-29.

The Raiders then scored two touchdowns in nine seconds and won 43-32. Most of the East saw a girl in the Alps.

After that night NBC put in "Heidi phones" on their own exchange, and the NFL wrote into its TV deals that games are shown to the end.

Our view: Timex got exactly what it paid for, to the second. The fans paid with the ending. Who pays for the slot decides what you see.
