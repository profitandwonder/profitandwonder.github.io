How did Apple pay 0.005% tax on its European profit?

When someone in Europe bought an iPhone, the sale was booked by an Apple company in Ireland. Two Irish tax rulings, from 1991 and 2007, said only its small Irish branch was taxed in Ireland. Most of the profit went to the company's "head office", which the EU Commission said "existed only on paper" and had no employees.

In 2014 the tax rate was 0.005%: about €50 on every €1 million of profit (our arithmetic on the EU's figure). It was legal under Ireland's rulings.

In 2016 the EU Commission called the rulings illegal state aid and ordered Ireland to collect up to €13 billion plus interest. Ireland fought on Apple's side. In September 2024 the EU's top court gave the final word: Ireland had to take the money. Almost €14.25 billion reached the Irish state, and Ireland is spending it on water, sewers and the power grid.
