Is the Blue Lagoon really a power plant's waste water?

Yes. The Blue Lagoon in Iceland began as runoff from the Svartsengi geothermal power plant. The plant pulls hot sea water from under the lava to make power. In the 1970s, the used water pooled in the lava field and turned milky blue.

In the 1980s, locals found that the water eased psoriasis, an itchy skin illness. In 1992, the company Blue Lagoon hf. started charging at the door. Today more than a million people a year visit. The plant's company, HS Orka, still supplies the hot water.

The lagoon sits next to a volcano. Eruptions closed it in 2023 and 2024, and lava took the car park. New walls now guard the plant and the lagoon.

In November 2026 the company sold shares on Nasdaq Iceland. About 4,000 investors wanted in, more than there were shares. The sale valued it at about $787 million.
